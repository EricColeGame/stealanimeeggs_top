### The three phases of progression

Most players move through roughly three phases, and the correct purchase changes at each one:

1. **Bootstrap phase** — you have almost nothing. Raw speed and capacity win because they increase how many attempts you get per minute.
2. **Compounding phase** — you have steady income. Multipliers now beat flat bonuses because they scale with everything else you own.
3. **Specialization phase** — you have a real roster. Upgrades that support your specific build outperform generic stat boosts.

| Phase | Primary focus | Secondary focus | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bootstrap | Steal speed | Carry capacity | Expensive luck upgrades |
| Compounding | Income multipliers | Capacity + cooldowns | Spreading across everything |
| Specialization | Build-specific boosts | Rarity upgrades | Generic flat bonuses |

Community reports consistently say the same thing: players who stall out are almost always stuck buying upgrades for the phase they *want* to be in rather than the phase they are actually in.

## Priority Order: Which Steal Anime Eggs Upgrades to Buy First

If you only remember one rule, remember this one: **buy the upgrade that increases the rate at which you can buy more upgrades.** That is usually steal speed or an income multiplier, not a flashy rarity boost.

Here is a practical order that works across most versions of the game:

- **First:** the cheapest steal-speed tiers. They are inexpensive, they stack, and they immediately increase your attempts per minute.
- **Second:** one or two carry-capacity tiers, just enough that you stop making constant return trips.
- **Third:** your first income multiplier. This is the point where your earnings start compounding instead of trickling.
- **Fourth:** cooldown reductions on whatever ability you spam most. Small percentages here feel disproportionately good.
- **Fifth:** rarity and luck upgrades. These are strong, but they are a *multiplier on quality*, not on quantity — they pay off best once you already have volume.
- **Last:** cosmetic or prestige-adjacent upgrades, unless the reset itself grants a permanent bonus.

## Common Mistakes That Slow Your Progression

Most stalled accounts are not unlucky. They are making one of a handful of repeatable errors.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Buying luck upgrades too early | Luck multiplies quality, not quantity — with low volume it barely registers | Max cheap speed and income tiers first |
| Spreading upgrades evenly | You end up with no meaningful multiplier in any category | Commit to one build for at least a full phase |
| Ignoring capacity | Constant return trips quietly eat a huge share of your session | Buy capacity in small, regular increments |
| Chasing the newest upgrade | New categories are often expensive per point of value | Compare cost-to-benefit before buying |
| Skipping resets or prestige | Long-term scaling usually lives behind the reset | Reset once the permanent bonus outweighs the temporary loss |
| Never checking cooldowns | A long cooldown caps your ceiling no matter how strong you are | Reduce cooldowns on your most-used ability |

Player experience across similar games suggests the "spreading evenly" mistake is the single biggest cause of mid-game slowdowns. It feels productive because every purchase does *something* — it just does not compound.

- **Re-evaluate after every reset.** A build that worked at your old power level may be the wrong build at your new one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What should I upgrade first in Steal Anime Eggs?**

Start with the cheapest steal-speed tiers, then add a small amount of carry capacity. Speed increases how many attempts you get per session, and attempts are the raw input for everything else. Only after your income is stable should you invest heavily in luck or rarity upgrades, since those multiply quality rather than quantity.

**Are luck and rarity upgrades worth it in Steal Anime Eggs upgrades builds?**

Yes, but timing matters more than the upgrade itself. Luck boosts shine once you are already stealing at a high volume, because they change the *distribution* of your pulls rather than the number of pulls. Buy them in the compounding phase, not the bootstrap phase, and pair them with capacity so you can hold what you find.

**How often should I reset or prestige?**

Reset when the permanent bonus you gain clearly outweighs the temporary progress you lose. In practice, that usually means resetting once your current run has slowed to a crawl and the next permanent tier is out of reach. Community reports suggest resetting too early is a smaller mistake than never resetting at all.

**Do Steal Anime Eggs upgrades carry over between sessions?**

Permanent upgrade tiers typically persist on your account, while temporary run-based bonuses usually reset with each run or prestige. Always check the in-game description before spending, because the wording distinguishes permanent multipliers from temporary ones — and mixing them up is an easy way to waste a large amount of currency.
