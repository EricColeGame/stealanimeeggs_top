## How to Read a Steal Anime Eggs Egg List

A raw list of names is close to useless. What makes an egg list actionable is the supporting data attached to each entry. Community-maintained sheets usually include a handful of standard columns, and each one answers a different question.

| Column | What It Shows | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Egg name | The in-game label for the egg | Lets you identify targets on sight |
| Rarity tier | Common through Mythic or Secret | Rough proxy for how hard it is to get |
| Hatch chance band | How often it appears from its source | Tells you whether to farm or trade |
| Base value | Starting worth before trading | The floor for any negotiation |
| Steal difficulty | How risky the grab is | Determines whether the attempt is worth it |
| Trade demand | How many players want it | Often matters more than raw rarity |
| Update status | Whether it is currently obtainable | Flags eggs that may be retired or reworked |

The single biggest mistake here is treating rarity and value as the same thing. They are related, but not identical. An egg can be extremely rare and still sit unwanted in your inventory because nobody is chasing it. Demand is what converts rarity into profit.

If you only track one extra column beyond the name, track demand. Community reports consistently show that high-demand mid-tier eggs trade faster than forgotten top-tier ones.

### Where Egg Lists Come From

There is no single official spreadsheet handed down by the developers. Most lists are assembled from player experience — people hatching hundreds of eggs, logging results, and comparing notes. That means two things for you:

- Numbers are estimates, not guarantees.
- Lists go stale the moment an update drops.

Treat every list as a snapshot. Verify anything critical in-game before you commit to a big trade or a long grind. ## Steal Anime Eggs Rarity Tiers at a Glance

Rarity tiers are the backbone of any egg list. Exact names differ between games and updates, but the structure is almost always the same ladder. The table below uses qualitative bands rather than hard numbers, because published odds shift with nearly every patch.

| Tier | Typical Hatch Rate | Relative Value | Steal Priority | Best Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Common | Very high | Lowest | Skip | Filling early inventory slots |
| Uncommon | High | Low | Low | Quick early-game currency |
| Rare | Moderate | Medium | Medium | Steady income, trade filler |
| Epic | Low | High | High | Primary steal targets |
| Legendary | Very low | Very high | Very high | Trade bait and upgrades |
| Mythic / Secret | Extremely low | Top tier | Top priority | Collection centerpiece |

A few practical notes on reading this table:

- **Commons are not worthless, they are just inefficient.** They are fine as starter material but should be traded up or discarded as soon as better options appear.
- **The Epic band is where most players should live.** It is reachable through normal play and still carries real trade weight.
- **Legendary and above are long-term projects.** Expect to combine farming, trading, and lucky steals rather than relying on any single method.
- **Tier names are not standardized.** One game's "Mythic" is another's "Godly." Match by behavior, not by label.

## Building Your Personal Egg List: A Six-Step Routine

Copying someone else's spreadsheet only gets you so far. Your collection, your play schedule, and your risk tolerance all shape which eggs you should chase. This routine takes about ten minutes and pays off for weeks.

| Step | Action | Time Needed | Why It Helps |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Inventory your current eggs | 3–5 min | You cannot plan trades without knowing your assets |
| 2 | Tag each egg with rarity and value | 2–3 min | Turns a messy inventory into a ranked list |
| 3 | Sort by steal value, not by rarity | 1 min | Surfaces the eggs actually worth chasing |
| 4 | Scout your target before attempting | Varies | Reduces wasted attempts and failed grabs |
| 5 | Steal, then secure immediately | Varies | Locking in a win beats gambling on a second one |
| 6 | Re-check after every update | 5 min | Keeps the list from going stale |

Step three is the one people skip. Sorting by rarity feels intuitive, but rarity alone ignores demand. An egg ranked fifth by rarity might be ranked first by trade value because it is newer, visually distinctive, or tied to a limited event.

### Keep a Backup Copy

Player experience suggests that shared spreadsheets get edited, locked, or abandoned regularly. Keep your own copy — a simple notes app or spreadsheet is enough — and update it yourself. That way an update never leaves you starting from zero.

## Steal Priority Rankings: What to Target First

Not every egg deserves the same effort. The ranking below groups targets by the risk-to-reward ratio most players report, and it works regardless of which specific eggs are currently in rotation.

| Rank | Target Profile | Risk Level | Expected Payoff | When to Attempt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S | Top-tier rarity with active demand | High | Highest | When you have a safe escape route |
| A | Epic and Legendary tiers | Medium-high | Strong | During low-traffic sessions |
| B | Solid mid-tier Rares | Medium | Moderate | As filler between big attempts |
| C | Commons and Uncommons | Low | Minimal | Almost never |

The logic behind the ranking is simple. S-tier eggs justify a risky attempt because a single success can reshape your inventory. C-tier eggs never justify risk, because even a clean steal barely changes your position.

A common community tip: attempt S-tier steals early in a session, when you are alert and the server is quieter. Save the low-stakes filler grabs for later, or skip them entirely.

## Mistakes That Ruin a Perfectly Good Egg List

Even a well-researched list fails if you use it wrong. These are the recurring errors that show up in player experience threads again and again.

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | The Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Chasing rarity over demand | You end up holding eggs nobody wants | Weight the demand column heavily |
| Ignoring update notes | Your list silently goes out of date | Re-verify after every patch |
| Hoarding every common | Inventory clutter hides your real assets | Trade up or clear out regularly |
| Attempting every steal | Wasted time and unnecessary risk | Follow your priority ranking |
| Trusting one source | Single lists carry single points of failure | Cross-check two or three sources |

Two more habits are worth building. First, screenshot your inventory before a big trade so you have a record if something goes wrong. Second, write down what you paid for each major egg — a running cost basis makes it much easier to tell a good trade from a bad one later.

## FAQ

**What is a Steal Anime Eggs egg list, exactly?**
It is a reference document that catalogs the eggs available in the game along with their rarity tier, approximate value, and other traits. Players use it to decide which eggs are worth farming, trading for, or stealing.

**How often should I update my egg list?**
After every major update, at minimum. New eggs get added, old ones get rebalanced, and hatch odds shift. A list that is two updates behind can actively mislead you.

**Are the rarity tiers the same in every game?**
No. Tier names vary widely, and the same word can mean different things across titles. Compare eggs by their actual behavior — hatch rate, demand, and trade value — rather than by the label attached to them.

**Is the highest-rarity egg always the best target?**
Not necessarily. Demand drives value more than rarity does. A mid-tier egg that everyone wants will often trade faster and for more than a top-tier egg that has fallen out of fashion.
